Salsa Sync v2.29

How it works

Prop your phone or laptop 2.5–3 m away, low down and tilted up, with your whole body (head to feet) in frame and the volume up. Tap Start camera once. After that you don't need to touch it: raise both hands above your head for 2 seconds to open the menu, then hold a hand over a button until it fills up. Raising both hands during a session or step check stops it.

The first session or step check after you turn the camera on starts with a body check: stand still for a few seconds so the app learns how much your feet wobble when they're not moving. Redo it (in Settings) if you move the camera. A session then shows a big 3, 2, 1, counts you in with "5, 6, 7, 8" blips and you dance two practice basics, starting on the green 1. A second, higher "5, 6, 7, 8" means the real thing starts on the next 1, and that part is recorded. Step check flashes L or R each time it sees a foot land.

Bluetooth headphones and speakers play the music late, which makes every step look late. Run Audio sync in Settings (or from the menu) once for each pair of headphones or speakers: march on the spot to 16 clicks and it measures the delay. Filming from slightly to one side helps when your feet cross in front of each other.

Music: tapping Start session lets you pick Beat only, the built-in Salsa band (any tempo) or a real song, plus the tempo and length. In the hands-up menu, Session opens the same choices as − and + buttons, and changing the music plays a few seconds of it. The real songs are free-to-share recordings from the Free Music Archive: "Cupido" and "Vamos A Tocar Sonero" by Frank Guerrero y Su Grupo Aché (CC BY-NC-ND 3.0, played unchanged at their own tempo) and "La Mujer de Antonio" by SONGO 21 (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, sped up or slowed down for each tempo). Audio sync for your headphones or speakers

Bluetooth headphones and speakers play the music late, which makes every step look late. Do this once for each pair of headphones or speakers, and redo it if you switch to different ones: march on the spot, one step on every click, and it measures the delay.

Step sensitivity for your camera and room

The first time you start after turning the camera on, a 3 second body check (stand still) measures how much the camera's points wobble and sets how high a foot must lift to count as a step. Redo it if you move the camera.
