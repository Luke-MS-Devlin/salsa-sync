Audio sync for your headphones or speakers

Bluetooth headphones and speakers play the music late, which makes every step look late. Do this once for each pair of headphones or speakers, and redo it if you switch to different ones: march on the spot, one step on every click, and it measures the delay.

Step sensitivity for your camera and room

The first time you start after turning the camera on, a 3 second body check (stand still) measures how much the camera's points wobble and sets how high a foot must lift to count as a step. Redo it if you move the camera.

How to use it

Prop your phone or laptop 2.5–3 m away, low down and tilted up, with your whole body (head to feet) in frame, the room well lit and the volume up. Tap the camera button once. After that you don't need to touch it: raise both hands above your head for 2 seconds to open the menu, then hold a hand over a button until it fills up. Raising both hands during a session or step check stops it.

The button at the bottom of the camera screen turns the camera on and off; with more than one camera, the button beside it switches between them (front and back on a phone). Sessions start from the hands-up menu; the music, tempo and length can also be chosen in Settings. On a phone, swipe left and right to move between the camera, Results, History, Settings and Help, or tap the round camera button to go straight back.

The first session after you turn the camera on starts with a body check: stand still for a few seconds so the app learns how much your feet wobble when they're not moving. Redo it (in Settings) if you move the camera. A session then shows a big 3, 2, 1, counts you in with "5, 6, 7, 8" blips and you dance two practice basics, starting on the green 1. A second, higher "5, 6, 7, 8" means the real thing starts on the next 1, and that part is recorded.

At the end the camera screen shows your score and one tip. The Results tab has the details: four scores (Tempo, Pause length, Timing, On the beat), your typical basic, which you can tap to see each score on it. The History tab has every session you've done on this device, with a chart of your score over time; tap one to see its results again.

Bluetooth headphones and speakers play the music late, which makes every step look late. Run Audio sync in Settings (or from the hands-up menu) once for each pair: march on the spot to 16 clicks and it measures the delay. Dim light makes the camera slow down and miss steps, so turn the main light on. Filming from slightly to one side helps when your feet cross.

Salsa basics

Salsa grew out of Cuban son and other Afro-Caribbean music, and took its name in New York in the 1960s and 70s. The music counts in eights: 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8.

The basic step is six steps over those eight counts: step on 1, 2, 3, hold on 4, step on 5, 6, 7, hold on 8. The holds are the pause this app measures. Dancing "On1" means your first step lands on the 1, which is what Salsa Sync checks. ("On2" styles break on the 2 instead.)

The clave, two wooden sticks, plays the pattern the whole band locks to; the cowbell marks the beat. In the built-in Salsa band you can hear both, plus conga, piano and bass.

About Salsa Sync

Built for the Hacktoberfest 2026 DEV Open-Source AI Challenge. Open source (MIT) at github.com/Luke-MS-Devlin/salsa-sync. Everything runs on your device: the video never leaves it.

Body tracking by Google's MediaPipe Pose Landmarker.
